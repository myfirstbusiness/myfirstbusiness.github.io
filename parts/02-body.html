The real problem

The information isn't the bottleneck.
Knowing which part applies to you is.

There are ten million videos about starting a business. You've probably watched forty of them. You still haven't started. That isn't a discipline problem — it's a targeting problem. Generic advice is generic because it has to work for everyone, which means it's optimized for nobody.

01

Everything contradicts everything. One guru says start with content. The next says content is a trap and you should cold call. Both are right — for different businesses, with different margins, at different stages. Neither tells you which one you are.

02

Advice is written for people who already have something. "Improve your conversion rate." You have no traffic. "Raise your prices." You have no customers. The whole internet skipped step zero.

03

You're told to "find your passion" and then sell it. Passion is an input, not a business model. Plenty of people love things nobody will pay for. Nobody teaches you how to check first.

04

Every free resource is bait for a paid one. The guide is a hook, the webinar is a pitch, the community is a funnel. So you learn to distrust free things — which is exactly why this one takes no email, no account, and sells nothing.

The method

Built on the part of business
that is actually universal.

Almost nothing in business is universal at the tactic level. Cold email works for B2B services and fails for retail. Paid ads work for high-margin products and kill low-margin ones. Anyone selling you a tactic that "works for every business" is selling you something. But there is a layer underneath that genuinely holds everywhere — and it's smaller than you think. Every playbook this site generates is built from it.

A business exists only where value created exceeds cost to create it.

LAW 02

Where this fits

What this is — and what it isn't.

IT IS

A targeting device

It removes the ninety-five percent of business advice that doesn't apply to you and hands you the five percent that does, sequenced, with a date attached to each step. That single act of filtering is worth more than another forty hours of watching.

IT ISN'T

A guarantee

No document makes you money. Reps make you money. The playbook tells you what to do in week one; you still have to do week one. The honest number to track is how many times you have asked a real human being for money. Nothing else predicts anything.

IT IS

Free because it should be

The gap between people who start businesses and people who don't is mostly access to a competent adult who will tell them the truth in order. That shouldn't cost $997. There is no paid tier, no course at the end, no call to book.

IT ISN'T

Financial, legal or tax advice

It's an educational framework. Registering a business, paying tax, signing contracts and taking on debt are decisions with real legal consequences that vary by country. Check those with someone qualified in your jurisdiction.

Questions

Fair things to be suspicious about.

What's the catch? Everything free online is bait.

Reasonable instinct, and usually correct. Here the catch is: there is no email capture, no account, no paid tier, and no product behind the free product. It runs as a static page on free hosting, which costs the operator nothing to keep online. The whole thing is one HTML file. If that ever changes, it'll say so on this page.

Do you store my answers?

No. The questionnaire runs entirely in your browser's memory. Nothing is sent to a server, because there is no server — just a file being served to you. Close the tab and the answers are gone. That's also why you have to download the PDF now rather than "check your email."

I'm 16 and I have literally zero dollars. Is this for me?

Yes — that's the design centre. Every model recommended can be started with no capital, and the questionnaire specifically asks about money and time so the plan matches reality rather than assuming a budget. Note that in most countries there are age rules around registering a business and signing contracts; the playbook flags where you'd want a parent or guardian involved.

How is this different from asking an AI chatbot?

A chatbot answers the question you asked. This asks you twelve questions first, scores your answers against ten defined models, and returns the same structured document every time for the same inputs — so it can't wander, flatter you, or invent a plan that sounds good and skips the cash math. It's a scoring engine with a document attached, not a conversation.

What if I get a recommendation I don't like?

Then take one of the two alternates, or redo the quiz answering more honestly about what you'd actually enjoy — question 9 and 11 move the result a lot. The recommendation is a starting point that gets you to a real customer fast, not a life sentence. Most people who build something successful have three to five dead attempts behind them.

Will this work in my country?

The universal laws and the models do. The specific mechanics — how you register, how you get paid, what taxes apply — vary enormously, and the playbook tells you what to look up rather than pretending to know your local rules. The outreach scripts and the 90-day plan are country-agnostic.

Can I redo it later?

Yes, as many times as you want, and you should — after your first customer, your answers to "capital", "skills" and "selling comfort" will all be different, and the plan should change with them. There's nothing to log into and nothing to cancel.
